## 1. Python only
- All analysis, data cleaning, charts, tables, and the script that builds the webpage are written in **Python**. No R, no SQL files, no JavaScript-built charts, no Excel formulas.

## 2. Reproducibility (graded: "another person can reproduce your results")
- One entry point regenerates everything from `data/Northwind Database.xlsx`: every number, figure and table on the webpage. Example: `python run_all.py`.
- Relative paths only. No hard-coded `/Users/...` paths, no manual steps, no hand-typed numbers in the HTML. Every figure in the page text is pulled from computed results.

## 3. Data handling (known quirks of this workbook)
- Sheets: Orders (830), Orders Details (2,155), Product (77), Customers (91), Suppliers (29), Employees (9), Categories (8), Shippers (3).
- Dates are Excel serial numbers. Convert with origin 1899-12-30.
- Revenue per line = `UnitPrice × Quantity × (1 − Discount)`, using the **order-line** UnitPrice, not `Product.Price`.
- Product has leftover columns from an earlier exercise (`Cheap` and `NewPrice` each twice, plus `IsCheap`). Ignore them unless a question needs them, and say so.
- The data covers **4 Jul 1996 – 6 May 1998**. 1996 and 1998 are partial years, and May 1998 has only 14 orders. Never compare raw yearly totals across partial years. Use full months, matching periods (e.g. Jan–Apr vs Jan–Apr), or run-rates, and state which.
- Shipped date can be missing (unshipped orders), and Region/Fax fields are often blank. Handle these explicitly and note it.
- The full data check is in `notes/data_check.md` (script: `scripts/01_data_check.py`; CSVs in `data/*.csv`). Key points:
  - Round `Discount` to 2 decimals.
  - Use `Customers.Country`, not `ShipCountry`, for customer markets: 4 orders have mixed-up ship-to addresses.
  - Exclude the 21 unshipped orders (Apr–May 1998) from delivery metrics only.
  - Prices rose about 25% during 1997: 1996 lines were sold at 80% of today's list price.

## 4. Verification before anything is reported
- After loading: check row counts against the list above, uniqueness of keys, and that every foreign key joins (no orphan OrderIDs or ProductIDs).
- Reconcile totals: revenue summed by any grouping must equal total revenue.
- Cross-check each headline number a second way (a different grouping or a quick independent calculation) before it goes on the page.
- Report findings with their size and caveats (sample size, partial periods, a single dominant customer, etc.). Do not overclaim causation from observational data.

## 5. Webpage content (graded)
- **Chosen questions (student's decision, 2026-11-24):**
  - **Q3** Which countries are growing or shrinking? Compare matching months.
  - **Q2** Which regular customers stopped ordering, and who should sales call first?
  - **Q4** How dependent are we on a few big customers?
  - Q1 (discount cap) is checked in `scripts/q1_discount_check.py` and is an optional extra. Details are in `notes/question_candidates.md`.
- **At least 3** meaningful business questions, each tied to a decision, opportunity, risk or operational understanding. Avoid "simple lookup" questions such as "Which product has the most orders?".
- Each question gets, in this order: **the question → why it matters → analytical approach → visualization or table → main finding → business implication**.
- At least one purposeful visualization per question, unless a table communicates the result better. Pick the chart type for the job (line for time, bar for ranking, scatter for relationships, box/histogram for distributions, heatmap for two dimensions).
- Every chart has a readable title, labeled axes **with units** (USD, orders, days…), legends where needed, and readable fonts.
- Audience is a manager: plain language, with the key number stated in the finding.
